IN SHORT

I run this tool on my own, for free, for guilds playing Foundation: Galactic Frontier. This page explains what I store about you, why, for how long, and how to get it changed or deleted.

The short version: I store your in-game name, your in-game player ID, your event statistics, and your Discord ID if you chose to link it. I do not store your real name, your address, your phone number, or any payment details, because there is nothing to pay for. I set no cookies and I run no analytics or advertising of any kind.

If you want your data deleted, write to fgfwiki@gmail.com and I will do it. Section 7 explains exactly what happens.

1. WHO IS RESPONSIBLE FOR YOUR DATA

I am. My name is André Vieira. I am the data controller for this service within the meaning of Article 4(7) of the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679, "the GDPR"). I operate from France, so French law and the GDPR both apply.

You can reach me at fgfwiki@gmail.com. That address is read by me and nobody else.

I have not appointed a Data Protection Officer. Article 37 of the GDPR does not require one here: I am one person, the processing is small in scale, and none of it involves large scale monitoring or special category data.

2. WHO THIS PAGE IS ABOUT

Two groups of people:

  • People who hold an account here. Guild leaders, officers, and players who signed in.
  • Players whose name appears in a guild roster. You do not need an account to be listed. A guild officer adds you and records your event participation. This page covers you too, and your rights are the same.

If you are in the second group and you would rather not be listed at all, tell your guild officers, or write to me directly. I will take you out.

3. WHAT I STORE

This is the full list. If something is not here, I do not have it.

  • Your account. An account name you or an officer chose, your role (player, guild admin, server admin), the guild and server it belongs to, and whatever is needed to sign you in. Sign in credentials are stored hashed or encrypted, and no one, including me, sees them in the interface.
  • Your in-game identity. Your in-game name and your in-game player ID (UID). I also keep a history of name changes, so your past statistics still match you after you rename.
  • Your activity in the game, as your guild records it. Event attendance, scores, power and rating values, declared absences, squad assignments, sanctions, and bans. Officers enter this by hand or by uploading a screenshot for automatic reading.
  • Your Discord ID and username, but only if you linked a Discord account. I ask Discord for your identity and nothing else. I do not request your email address and I do not request the list of servers you are in.
  • Whatever you type into free text fields. Bug reports, wiki pages, and comments. Please do not put anything private in them.
  • The IP address you sign in from, kept briefly to stop people hammering the login page. I do not use it to work out where you are, and I do not build any profile from it.
  • Crash reports. When something breaks in your browser, a report may be sent to the error monitoring service named in Section 6. It contains the error, the part of the code that failed, and which guild was open. Passwords and tokens are stripped out before anything leaves your browser.

Screenshots are not kept. When an officer uploads a screenshot of the member list so it can be read automatically, the image is processed and discarded. It is never written to a disk or a storage bucket. What stays is the text that was read out of it, plus a fingerprint of the image so that sending the same screenshot twice does not cost a second reading.

Yes, this is personal data. An in-game name, a player ID and a Discord ID are all online identifiers under Article 4(1) of the GDPR, and an IP address counts as personal data too. Earlier versions of these pages claimed the tool held no personal information at all. That was wrong, and I have withdrawn it.

4. WHAT I NEVER ASK FOR

Real names. Postal addresses. Phone numbers. Dates of birth. Identity documents. Bank or card details, since the service is free and has no way to take a payment. Precise location or any device GPS data. Nothing that Article 9 of the GDPR treats as sensitive, such as health, religion, politics or anything similar. I have no use for any of it.

5. WHY I STORE IT, AND WHAT ALLOWS ME TO

Every purpose below rests on one of the legal grounds in Article 6(1) of the GDPR.

  • Running the tool for your guild. Keeping the roster, the events, the scores and the statistics so a guild can organise itself. Legal ground: performance of a contract (Article 6(1)(b)) if you accepted the Terms when you got an account. For players who never signed up, it is the legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)) of the guild and its officers in managing their own roster.
  • Signing you in and keeping accounts separate. Checking who you are, ending sessions when a password changes, and making sure one guild cannot read another guild’s data. Legal ground: performance of a contract, and my legitimate interest in keeping the service secure.
  • Stopping abuse. The short lived record of sign in attempts and the IP they came from. Legal ground: legitimate interest in protecting the service and the people using it from password guessing and flooding.
  • Fixing crashes. Legal ground: legitimate interest in running something that works. Reports are stripped of credentials, and the service that receives them is told not to add your IP address.
  • Reading screenshots automatically. So officers do not retype a hundred names. Legal ground: legitimate interest. The image itself is not kept.
  • Moderation. Sanctions and bans recorded by a guild’s own officers. Legal ground: the legitimate interest of a guild in deciding who is in it.
  • Keeping an audit trail. A log of administrative actions, so a change can be traced and disputed. Legal ground: legitimate interest in accountability.

Nothing here relies on your consent, so there is no consent for you to withdraw. Nothing here makes automated decisions about you with legal or similarly significant effects, so Article 22 of the GDPR does not come into play. Where I rely on legitimate interest, you can object at any time: see Section 7.

Data leaving the European Union. Some of these companies are based in the United States. Where data reaches them, the transfer is covered by the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses and, where the company is certified, by the EU to US Data Privacy Framework. This is set out in each company’s data processing agreement. You can ask me for the details.

Inside the tool itself, guilds are kept apart. An officer sees their own guild and no other. That separation is enforced inside the database, not just hidden in the interface, so it holds even if someone goes round the website and talks to the server directly.

7. YOUR RIGHTS, AND HOW TO USE THEM

Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR give you the right to see the data I hold about you, to have it corrected, to have it deleted, to limit what I do with it, to object to my using it, and to get a copy in a portable format. You can also complain to a supervisory authority.

How to ask. Write to fgfwiki@gmail.com. Tell me your in-game name and your guild, or your account name. There is nothing to pay.

How long I take. One month from when your message arrives, as Article 12(3) of the GDPR requires. If a request is complicated I can take up to two months longer, but I have to tell you inside the first month, with the reason.

Checking it is really you. I hold no email address for players, so I may have to confirm who you are through your guild’s Discord or through one of its officers before I act. This is to stop one player deleting another player’s record. I will not ask you for anything more than what that check needs, and certainly not for an identity document.

What deletion actually does. For a player: your in-game name is replaced everywhere by a meaningless code, generated from a random value that is thrown away immediately, so it cannot be traced back or matched with anything. Your player ID and your declared timezone are erased. Your absences, your name history and your transfer requests are deleted outright. Your roster entry stays, but as an anonymous placeholder with nothing in it that points to you, because your guild’s historical totals are attached to it. Once that is done, those records are no longer personal data under Recital 26 of the GDPR, and I am allowed to keep them. For an account: the account and everything needed to sign in are deleted, and your name is replaced by the same kind of code on any wiki page or comment you wrote.

One exception, and I would rather state it than hide it. If a guild has recorded a ban against you, that record is kept as it is, player ID included. A ban list cannot do its job without knowing who is banned. Article 17(3) of the GDPR allows this, and Article 17(1)(c) lets me weigh a guild’s interest against your objection. If you think a ban record about you should go, write to me: I will look at it properly and tell you what I decided and why.

8. HOW LONG I KEEP THINGS

These are enforced by jobs that run on a schedule, not by me remembering to do it.

  • Sign in IP addresses and attempt counters: 24 hours. Deleted every hour.
  • Sign in codes, one time tickets and link codes: until they expire, which is a matter of minutes, then deleted every hour. A link code that was used is kept as a record of who used it and when.
  • Text read out of screenshots: until it expires, then deleted every hour.
  • Guild creation requests: 14 days if nobody acts on them. The Discord handle attached to a closed request is removed 90 days after it closes.
  • Administrative audit log: 12 months.
  • Name change history: 12 months.
  • Rosters, events, scores and statistics: for as long as the guild keeps using the tool. A guild that empties out is removed automatically. These records are also deleted on request, as described in Section 7.
  • Accounts: until deleted, either at your request or by an administrator with authority over the account.

Where a period is given in months, it is a maximum. Ask and I will delete sooner.

9. COOKIES, AND WHAT SITS IN YOUR BROWSER

There are no cookies on this site. None at all. There is no advertising, no analytics, no tag manager, and no tracking pixel.

The site does use your browser’s local storage, for things it cannot work without: which session you are signed into, which guild and tab you are looking at, and a few display preferences. None of it goes to anyone else and none of it is used to follow you around. Article 5(3) of Directive 2002/58/EC exempts storage that is strictly necessary for a service you asked for, which is why you are not being shown a consent banner. Clearing your browser data removes all of it and signs you out.

If I ever add something that is not strictly necessary, I will ask you first, properly, and it will be off until you say yes.

10. KEEPING IT SAFE, AND WHAT HAPPENS IF I FAIL

Guilds are separated inside the database itself, so one guild cannot read another’s records even if someone bypasses the website. Sign in credentials are hashed or encrypted at rest. Traffic is encrypted in transit and the site runs under a strict content security policy. Credentials and tokens are stripped from logs and crash reports before they leave the application. The whole platform was audited in September 2026 and the findings were fixed.

If there is ever a breach that is likely to put your rights at risk, I will tell the CNIL within 72 hours as Article 33 of the GDPR requires, and I will tell you directly where Article 34 requires it. I will say what happened rather than write around it.

11. CHILDREN

This tool is not aimed at children. Accounts are held by guild officers, and the player names in it are put there by them. If you believe a child’s data is stored here, write to the address in Section 7 and I will delete it.

12. IF YOU WANT TO COMPLAIN

Please write to me first. I would rather fix it than have you go elsewhere, and I am the only person who can actually change anything here.

You do not have to, though. You can complain to the French data protection authority, the Commission Nationale de l’Informatique et des Libertés (CNIL), 3 Place de Fontenoy, TSA 80715, 75334 Paris Cedex 07, France, www.cnil.fr. If you live or work in another EU country, you can go to your own national authority instead.
