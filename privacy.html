PURPOSE OF THIS POLICY

This Policy is the information notice required by Articles 13 and 14 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (the “GDPR”) and by Law No. 78-17 of 6 January 1978. It states what personal data the FGF Guild Management Tool processes, on what legal basis, for how long, by whom, and how you exercise your rights.

It forms an integral part of the Terms of Use.

In short: the Service stores in-game pseudonyms and identifiers, event statistics, and a Discord identifier where you have linked one. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics and displays no advertising. To have your data erased, write to fgfwiki@gmail.com.

1. CONTROLLER

1.1. The controller within the meaning of Article 4(7) GDPR is André Vieira, a natural person established in France, contactable at fgfwiki@gmail.com. That address is monitored by the controller alone and is the single point of contact for every request under this Policy.

1.2. No data protection officer. None has been designated. Article 37(1) GDPR does not require one: the controller is not a public authority, the processing does not consist of operations requiring regular and systematic monitoring of data subjects on a large scale, and it does not consist of large-scale processing of the special categories of data referred to in Article 9 or of data relating to criminal convictions.

1.3. Guild administrators. Where a guild administrator decides, on their own initiative, which players to record and what to record about them, they determine the purposes and means of that processing and are a controller in their own right for it, within the meaning of Article 4(7) GDPR. The operator remains the controller of the platform. A request addressed to the operator is forwarded to the administrator concerned where it falls to them.

2. DATA SUBJECTS

This Policy concerns, first, persons holding an account on the Service, and second, players recorded in a guild roster who hold no account. The second group have not themselves provided their data; Article 14 GDPR applies to them, and their rights are identical. A player who does not wish to appear may say so to their guild administrators or write directly to the address in Article 1.1.

3. CATEGORIES OF DATA PROCESSED

3.1. The following list is exhaustive.

  • Account data: account identifier, role, guild and server of attachment, authentication material. Authentication material is stored hashed or encrypted and is never displayed.
  • In-game identity: in-game pseudonym, in-game player identifier (UID), and the history of pseudonym changes, so that statistics remain attributable after a rename.
  • Game activity: event attendance, scores, power and rating values, declared absences, squad assignments, sanctions and bans, entered by guild administrators or transcribed from screenshots they upload.
  • Discord identifiers, where the data subject has linked a Discord account: Discord user identifier and username. The authorisation requests the identify scope alone; no email address and no server list are requested. Answering a call to arms in a guild’s Discord server also records the Discord user identifier with the answer given, whether or not an account is linked.
  • Free text entered by the data subject: support tickets, wiki pages, comments.
  • Connection IP address, recorded transiently for the sole purpose of limiting sign-in attempts. It is used for no analytics, profiling or geolocation.
  • Technical diagnostics: where the application encounters an error, a report may be transmitted to the processor identified in Article 6, comprising the error, the code path and the identifier of the active guild. Credentials and tokens are stripped before transmission.

3.2. Screenshots. Images uploaded for automated transcription are processed in volatile memory and are not retained: no disk persistence and no object storage. What is retained is the transcribed text and a cryptographic fingerprint of the image, so that an identical resubmission need not be transcribed again.

3.3. Never collected. Civil names, postal addresses, telephone numbers, dates of birth, identity documents, banking or payment data, precise location data, and any special category of data within the meaning of Article 9 GDPR or data relating to criminal convictions within the meaning of Article 10.

3.4. Qualification. An in-game pseudonym, a player identifier and a Discord identifier are online identifiers expressly contemplated by Article 4(1) GDPR, and an IP address constitutes personal data (CJEU, 19 October 2016, Breyer, C-582/14). The Service therefore processes personal data. Any earlier statement that it held none is withdrawn.

4. PURPOSES AND LEGAL BASES

Each purpose rests on a single legal basis under Article 6(1) GDPR.

  • Operating the Service for a guild. Basis: performance of a contract, Article 6(1)(b), for account holders who accepted the Terms. For players holding no account: the legitimate interests of the guild and its administrators in managing their own roster, Article 6(1)(f).
  • Authentication and partitioning between guilds. Basis: performance of a contract, and legitimate interests in the security of the Service.
  • Preventing abuse (transient record of sign-in attempts and the associated IP address). Basis: legitimate interests in protecting the Service and its users against credential-stuffing and flooding. Recital 49 GDPR expressly recognises this interest.
  • Diagnosing errors. Basis: legitimate interests in operating a functioning service.
  • Automated transcription of screenshots. Basis: legitimate interests in sparing administrators from manual re-entry. The image is not retained.
  • Moderation records (sanctions, bans). Basis: the legitimate interests of a guild in governing its own membership.
  • Audit trail of administrative actions. Basis: legitimate interests in accountability and in the security of the processing, Article 32 GDPR.

4.1. Balancing. Where the basis is legitimate interests, the controller has carried out the balancing required by Article 6(1)(f). The data are limited to what a guild roster inherently contains, they are not combined with any external source, they are not transferred for consideration, and the data subject may object at any time under Article 7.1 below.

4.2. No processing relies on consent, so there is no consent to withdraw. No processing produces a decision based solely on automated processing producing legal effects or similarly significantly affecting the data subject within the meaning of Article 22 GDPR.

5. RETENTION PERIODS

Periods are enforced by scheduled database jobs, in accordance with the storage-limitation principle of Article 5(1)(e) GDPR.

  • Sign-in IP addresses and attempt counters: 24 hours, purged hourly.
  • Sign-in codes, one-time tickets and linking codes: until expiry (a matter of minutes), purged hourly. A redeemed linking code is retained as evidence of its use.
  • Transcribed screenshot text: until expiry, purged hourly.
  • Call-to-arms answers in Discord: 7 days, purged daily.
  • Guild creation requests: 14 days where unactioned; the Discord handle is erased 90 days after closure.
  • Administrative audit log: 12 months.
  • Pseudonym change history: 12 months.
  • Rosters, events, scores and statistics: for as long as the guild uses the Service. A guild that becomes empty is removed by a daily job. These records are erased on request under Article 7.
  • Accounts: until deleted, at the data subject’s request or by an administrator with authority over the account.

Periods expressed in months are maxima. Erasure may be obtained sooner on request.

A copy of the safeguards may be requested at the address in Article 1.1.

6.3. Partitioning. Access is partitioned by guild. An administrator accesses their own guild alone. This partitioning is enforced at database level and not merely in the interface, and therefore holds even where the application layer is bypassed.

7. RIGHTS OF DATA SUBJECTS

7.1. Rights. You have the right of access (Article 15), rectification (Article 16), erasure (Article 17), restriction of processing (Article 18), data portability (Article 20) and objection (Article 21), and the right to give instructions concerning the fate of your data after your death (Article 85 of Law No. 78-17).

7.2. Exercise. Write to fgfwiki@gmail.com, stating your in-game pseudonym and guild, or your account identifier. No charge applies.

7.3. Time limit. A reply is given within one month of receipt, in accordance with Article 12(3) GDPR. That period may be extended by two further months where the request is complex, in which case you are informed within the first month together with the reasons.

7.4. Verification of identity. The Service holds no email address for players. Where there is reasonable doubt as to the identity of the requester, the controller may seek confirmation through the guild’s Discord or through one of its administrators, in accordance with Article 12(6) GDPR. This is to prevent one player from obtaining the erasure of another’s records. No identity document is requested.

7.5. Effect of erasure. For a player: the in-game pseudonym is replaced throughout by a token derived from a random value generated for that erasure and immediately discarded, so that it can be neither reversed nor correlated; the player identifier and the declared timezone are erased; absences, pseudonym history and transfer requests are deleted. The roster entry subsists as an unattributable placeholder, since the participation and statistical records are attached to it. Those records, once deprived of any means of identification, no longer constitute personal data within the meaning of Recital 26 GDPR and may lawfully be retained. For an account: the account and all associated authentication material are deleted, and the author name on any wiki page or comment is replaced by a token.

7.6. Ban records: reasoned exception. Where a guild has recorded a ban, that record is retained in full, including the identifier. Such a list cannot serve its purpose without it. This retention is founded on Article 17(3) GDPR and on the balancing required by Article 17(1)(c), the guild’s legitimate interest in governing its membership prevailing, in this specific case, over the objection. Any person concerned may nonetheless contest that assessment at the address in Article 1.1; the controller will carry out the balancing afresh and give a reasoned reply.

8. TERMINAL STORAGE AND ABSENCE OF COOKIES

8.1. The Service places no cookie. It embeds no advertising, analytics, tag manager or tracking pixel.

8.2. It uses the browser’s local storage for operations strictly necessary to the service you have requested: session, active guild and tab, display preferences. None of it is transmitted to a third party or used for tracking. Such storage falls within the exemption in the second sentence of Article 5(3) of Directive 2002/58/EC, transposed at Article 82 of Law No. 78-17, which is why no consent banner is displayed. Clearing your browsing data removes it and signs you out.

8.3. Should any non-essential tracer be introduced, prior consent will be sought in accordance with Article 82 and it will remain inactive until consent is given.

9. SECURITY AND PERSONAL DATA BREACHES

9.1. Measures. In accordance with Article 32 GDPR: partitioning between guilds enforced at database level; authentication material hashed or encrypted at rest; encryption in transit; a strict content security policy; removal of credentials and tokens from logs and error reports; and an audit trail of administrative actions. The platform underwent a full security audit in September 2026 and the findings were remediated.

9.2. Breaches. Any personal data breach likely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons is notified to the CNIL within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, in accordance with Article 33 GDPR, and the data subjects concerned are informed where Article 34 so requires.

10. CHILDREN

The Service is not directed at children and offers no service of the information society directly to a child within the meaning of Article 8 GDPR: accounts are held by guild administrators, who themselves enter the pseudonyms recorded. Should a child’s data be held, erasure may be obtained at the address in Article 1.1.
