PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT

This Policy explains what personal data the FGF Guild Management Platform (the “Platform”) processes, why, on what legal basis, for how long, who else processes it, and how You exercise Your rights. It supplements and forms part of the Terms of Service.

The Platform is provided free of charge. It holds no payment data of any kind and operates no payment surface.

SECTION 1. CONTROLLER AND SCOPE

1.1. Data Controller: The controller is André Vieira (the “Operator”), reachable at fgfwiki@gmail.com. The Operator is an individual operating the Platform on a non-commercial, free-of-charge basis from the French Republic.

1.2. No Data Protection Officer: Given the scale and nature of the processing, the Operator has not appointed a Data Protection Officer. Article 37 GDPR does not require one here. All requests go to the address above.

1.3. Who this concerns: guild administrators who hold an account on the Platform, and players whose in-game pseudonym and statistics are recorded by their guild’s administrators. A player need not hold an account to appear in a roster; this Policy applies to them all the same.

SECTION 2. WHAT DATA IS PROCESSED

2.1. Never collected. The Platform does not collect real-world legal names, postal addresses, telephone numbers, dates of birth, government identifiers, payment or banking data, biometric data, special-category data under Article 9 GDPR, or device or hardware geolocation. It sets no cookies and embeds no advertising, analytics, tag-manager or tracking-pixel service.

2.2. Collected. Exhaustively, by category:

  • Account data — an account identifier, a role, the guild and server it belongs to, and authentication material. Authentication material is stored in hashed or encrypted form and is never displayed back to anyone.
  • In-game identity — the in-game pseudonym and the in-game player identifier (UID), plus the history of pseudonym changes so that statistics stay attributable when a player renames.
  • Game activity — event participation, scores, power and rating values, absences, squad assignments, sanctions and ban records, all entered by guild administrators or transcribed from screenshots they upload.
  • Discord identifiers — for Users who link a Discord account: the Discord user identifier and username. No email address, no server list, and no message content is requested.
  • Free text You write — support ticket descriptions, wiki pages and comments. Only enter what You are willing to have stored.
  • Sign-in IP address — recorded transiently for rate limiting and abuse prevention. It is not used for analytics, profiling or geolocation.
  • Technical diagnostics — when the application encounters an error, a report may be transmitted to the error-monitoring processor named in Section 5. It carries the error, the code path, and the identifier of the active guild. Credentials and tokens are stripped before transmission.

2.3. Screenshots. Roster screenshots uploaded for automated transcription (OCR) are processed in memory and are not stored: no disk persistence, no object storage. What is retained is the transcribed text and a cryptographic hash of the image, so that re-submitting the same screenshot need not be transcribed twice. The image itself is never written down.

2.4. This is personal data. An in-game pseudonym, a player UID and a Discord identifier are online identifiers within the meaning of Article 4(1) GDPR, and an IP address is personal data. The Platform therefore processes personal data, and this Policy governs it. Any earlier statement that the Platform held “zero personally identifiable information” was inaccurate and has been withdrawn.

SECTION 3. PURPOSES AND LEGAL BASES

Each purpose rests on one legal basis under Article 6(1) GDPR:

  • Providing the service to a guild — recording rosters, events, scores and statistics so a guild can organise itself. Legal basis: performance of a contract (Article 6(1)(b)) for account holders who accepted the Terms; legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)) of the guild and its administrators in managing their own roster, for players who do not hold an account.
  • Authentication and account security — verifying who is signing in, revoking sessions, maintaining role separation between tenants. Legal basis: performance of a contract, and legitimate interests in securing the service.
  • Abuse prevention and rate limiting — the transient record of sign-in IP addresses and attempt counters. Legal basis: legitimate interests in protecting the service and its users against credential-stuffing and flooding.
  • Error monitoring and reliability — diagnosing crashes. Legal basis: legitimate interests in operating a functioning service. Reports are stripped of credentials, and the processor is instructed not to enrich them with the sender’s IP address.
  • Automated transcription of screenshots (OCR) — saving administrators from retyping rosters. Legal basis: legitimate interests; the image is not retained.
  • Moderation records — bans and sanctions recorded by a guild’s administrators. Legal basis: legitimate interests of the guild in governing its own membership.
  • Audit trail — a log of administrative actions, so that a change can be attributed and disputed. Legal basis: legitimate interests in accountability and security.

No processing on this Platform relies on consent, and none involves automated decision-making producing legal effects within the meaning of Article 22 GDPR.

SECTION 4. HOW LONG DATA IS KEPT

Retention is enforced by scheduled database jobs, not by intention:

  • Sign-in IP addresses and rate-limit counters — 24 hours, deleted by an hourly job.
  • Sign-in and account-linking codes, one-time tickets and OAuth states — until they expire (minutes), then deleted by an hourly job. A redeemed account-linking code is kept as evidence of who used it and when.
  • Transcribed screenshot text (OCR cache) — until it expires, then deleted by an hourly job.
  • Pending guild-creation requests — 14 days, after which they are marked expired; the Discord handle they carry is removed 90 days after the request is closed.
  • Administrative audit log — 12 months.
  • Pseudonym-change history — 12 months.
  • Rosters, events, scores and statistics — for as long as the guild continues to use the Platform. A guild that becomes empty is expired and removed by a daily job. These records are also erased on request, as described in Section 6.
  • Accounts — until deleted, whether by the account holder’s request or by a guild administrator with authority over that account.

Where a period is stated in months, it is a maximum; data may be erased sooner on request.

SECTION 5. PROCESSORS AND RECIPIENTS

5.1. Personal data is never sold, rented, or disclosed for advertising. It is disclosed only to the following processors, each acting on the Operator’s instructions:

  • Supabase — database, authentication and server-side functions. Holds all of the data described in Section 2.
  • Vercel — hosting and content delivery of the web application.
  • Google (Gemini API) — automated transcription of roster screenshots. Receives the screenshot for the duration of the request; see Section 2.3.
  • Discord — account linking, identity verification, and delivery of notifications to a guild’s own Discord channels. Receives Discord identifiers and the content of the notifications a guild chooses to send.
  • Sentry — error monitoring, on its European infrastructure. Receives the diagnostic reports described in Section 2.2.
  • Render — hosting of the Discord verification worker.

5.2. Transfers outside the European Union. Some of these processors are established in, or operate infrastructure in, the United States. Where that is the case the transfer is governed by the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses and, where applicable, the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework, under the terms of each processor’s data processing agreement.

5.3. Within the Platform. Access is partitioned by guild. A guild administrator sees their own guild’s data and no other’s; this is enforced in the database itself, not only in the interface. A player sees their own record.

SECTION 6. YOUR RIGHTS

6.1. Under Articles 15 to 22 GDPR You have the right to obtain access to Your data, to have it rectified, to have it erased, to restrict or object to its processing, and to receive it in a portable format. You may also lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority.

6.2. How to exercise them. Write to fgfwiki@gmail.com, stating Your in-game pseudonym and guild, or Your account identifier. The Operator replies within one month of receipt, extendable by two further months for complex requests, in which case You will be told within the first month. There is no charge.

6.3. Identity. Because the Platform holds no email address for players, the Operator may need to verify a request through the guild’s own Discord channel or through a guild administrator before acting on it. This is to stop one player from deleting another’s records, and no more identifying information is requested than is necessary for that check.

6.4. What erasure does. Erasure is carried out by an explicit, logged procedure. For a player: the in-game pseudonym is replaced throughout by a token derived from a random value that is generated for that erasure and then discarded, so the original cannot be recovered or matched; the player identifier and the declared timezone are cleared; absences, pseudonym history and transfer requests are deleted outright. The roster entry itself remains as an unattributable placeholder carrying no identifier, because the participation and statistics records are linked to it — this is what allows those records to be kept in anonymised form, so a guild’s historical totals do not silently change. Anonymised records are no longer personal data; Article 11(2) and Recital 26 GDPR permit their retention. For an account: the account and all of its authentication material are deleted, and the author name on any wiki page or comment it wrote is replaced by a token.

6.5. The one exception: ban records. If a guild has recorded a ban against a player, that record is kept intact, identifier included. A ban list exists to stop a specific player rejoining, and it cannot do that without the identifier. Article 17(3) and the overriding-legitimate-grounds test in Article 17(1)(c) GDPR permit this, and it is stated here rather than applied silently. If You object to a ban record held about You, write to the address in Section 6.2: the Operator will weigh Your objection against the guild’s interest and will tell You the outcome.

6.6. Supervisory authority. The competent authority is the Commission Nationale de l’Informatique et des Libertés (CNIL), 3 Place de Fontenoy, TSA 80715, 75334 Paris Cedex 07, France — www.cnil.fr. You may also complain to the authority of Your own country of residence or place of work.

SECTION 7. STORAGE IN YOUR BROWSER, AND SECURITY

7.1. No cookies. The Platform sets no cookies. It uses Your browser’s local storage to remember strictly necessary things: Your session, which guild and tab You are viewing, and display preferences. None of it is transmitted to a third party and none of it is used for tracking. Under Article 5(3) of Directive 2002/58/EC this storage is exempt from the consent requirement, which is why no consent banner is shown. Clearing Your browser’s site data removes it and signs You out.

7.2. Security measures. Access is partitioned in the database itself so that one guild cannot read another’s records. Authentication material is hashed or encrypted at rest. Traffic is encrypted in transit and the site is served under a strict Content Security Policy. Credentials and tokens are stripped from logs and from error reports before they leave the application.

7.3. Breach notification. In the event of a personal data breach likely to result in a risk to Your rights and freedoms, the Operator will notify the CNIL within 72 hours as required by Article 33 GDPR and will inform affected Users where Article 34 requires it.

SECTION 8. CHILDREN, AND CHANGES TO THIS POLICY

8.1. Age. The Platform is not directed at children. Accounts are held by guild administrators, and the Platform records in-game pseudonyms supplied by them. If You believe a child’s data is held here, write to the address in Section 6.2 and it will be erased.

8.2. Changes. This Policy may be amended to reflect changes in the Platform or in the law. The Effective Date at the head of this document records the current version. Material changes will be announced through the guild Discord channels, which is the only channel by which the Operator can reach Users.
